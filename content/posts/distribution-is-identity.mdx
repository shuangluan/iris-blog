Last week I published a note on this blog: *My most reliable cofounder is AI*.

Today I audited where it lives now:

- Blog ✓
- Reddit /founder ✓
- LinkedIn ✗
- X ✗
- Red Notes ✗
- WeChat public account ✗
- Friend circle ✗

Seeing the list, I was a little startled.

## The obvious explanations are wrong

The story I first told myself was — "no time," "too much work," "I'll set up a workflow one day and then post."

But I've had time this week. The tools all exist. Buffer, Hootsuite, Publer — any of them will fan out a piece to five platforms with one click. **Time and tools aren't what's stopping me.**

## Platform by platform: what actually stopped me

**Reddit (posted in 3 minutes):** I pasted the first few paragraphs into r/founder with a one-line intro and hit submit. Three minutes total. I was confident the community would get it — the subject (how hard it is to find a real cofounder) is exactly what fellow founders talk about daily. They were going to nod.

**LinkedIn (didn't post):** Opened LinkedIn. Started thinking, "which posture do I take to post this here?" Closed LinkedIn.

**Red Notes/Xiaohongshu (didn't post):** Started thinking about the hook, the phrasing, the required visual. Got tired before I started. Closed the app.

**X (didn't post):** Account is 4 year old. Zero followers. Don't know if this should be a long single tweet or a thread or a screenshot post. I haven't figured out the rules, so I don't post.

**WeChat public account (haven't set one up):** Not because I can't. Because the content would be identical to the blog and I couldn't articulate the value of maintaining a second surface. (Separate post.)

**Friend circle (didn't post):** I only post milestones there. This is a note, not a milestone.

## The pattern

Look at the five "no"s. **None of them is a "writing workload" problem.**

- **Reddit I posted** because I knew who I was on that platform — a fellow solo builder talking to fellow solo builders.
- **LinkedIn / X / Xiaohongshu I didn't post** because I didn't know who I was *supposed to be* on those platforms.

**Distribution isn't a content problem. It's an identity problem.**

Every platform demands a different version of you. The friction isn't "reshaping the piece for Xiaohongshu takes 30 minutes." The friction is that **you have to first decide "who am I on Xiaohongshu?" before you can even sit down to write**. That decision is what paralyzes people.

## LinkedIn, zoomed in

LinkedIn is the sharpest case. Let me pull it out.

LinkedIn carries my history at ByteDance and TikTok. I know the audience there is other PMs, potential collaborators, VCs, people doing professional verification on me — they come here to check.

So on LinkedIn I feel I **should be**: someone who consistently outputs deep thinking, whose posts carry weight, who can represent a professional field as a senior PM.

In reality I feel I **am**: someone who just left big tech, still figuring things out. My previous titles make me look like a strong PM, but **titles are just titles**. I'm solo now, haven't shipped anything shiny yet. I want the industry recognition, want to be an expert in some field — but I know how shallow my actual knowledge base is.

The gap between those two versions of me is exactly what stops me from posting on LinkedIn. **The problem isn't that I can't write the piece — it's that I don't want to expose the "still learning" version of me on the platform where credential-checkers land.**

Xiaohongshu is a version of the same problem: the platform wants aesthetic, curated, tastefully-lived people; I'm someone who spends all day debugging in front of a screen. X is another version: the platform rewards accounts with a following and a clear POV; I'm a new account that hasn't figured out its rules.

**Every platform is asking me to be a version of me that I'm not.**

## The existing tools all miss this layer

Buffer, Hootsuite, Typefully all solve the **mechanical cost of pushing one piece to many surfaces**. They assume: **you already have a version for each platform; you just don't want to copy-paste one by one**.

That's not my problem. **I'm not tired of copy-pasting. I'm scared of deciding "who am I on this platform."**

One-click cross-posting is only useful for people who've already resolved the persona question for every platform. For someone like me, still gambling on a persona each time, one-click distribution amplifies the decision cost — once you press it you can't take it back — so I freeze harder than before.

## What I actually need

Not a cross-post button. Not "give me the LinkedIn version of this piece."

Something that looks at my draft and tells me:

> "On LinkedIn, this version will work. Here's why: you avoided X and Y in the draft — that's the vulnerability you're hiding — but if you kept Z it would actually feel more real. You're worried about being questioned as 'not senior enough,' but this platform is currently discussing A and B and your angle fits right in. Try this edit?"

**I need an agent that handles the persona anxiety for me, not a tool that handles the text.**

Rewriting text is cheap now — any modern LLM can do it. The expensive thing is helping me judge: *is this version of me safe on this platform, is it going to land, is it going to backfire?* Nobody's built that.

## The 20-minute vision

Fast forward six months. Twenty minutes after finishing a note, my flow looks like this:

I open one page — maybe my blog's admin, maybe a separate tool. I pick which platforms to publish to. AI goes to work — **on the same page**, columns come back for each platform: LinkedIn version, Xiaohongshu version, X thread, WeChat version, friend-circle version.

Under each column, AI's notes: why this version, what I'm betting the reader will pick up, what the risks are.

I read through. Per column, I hit approve or re-edit. The re-edits regenerate. Once everything is approved, I ship it all in one go.

Results roll back to the same page: LinkedIn shipped, Xiaohongshu pending because the image isn't generated yet, X errored on API, WeChat draft delivered.

**The key: I never have to leave that one page to check or edit or verify anything. Every decision and verification happens in one place.**

## What I'm building

I'm building the v0 of this for myself. I'm the most acute user.

I won't try to solve every platform at once. I'll pick the one that hurts the most — probably LinkedIn — and start with the "agent judges persona safety" piece. I'll use it every time I post to LinkedIn myself. Then extend to the next platform.

**If you're the person who spends 40 minutes editing a LinkedIn draft in the box before closing it, or the one who cringes at the thought of Xiaohongshu, or the one with a 0-follower X account who doesn't know where to start — reply and I'll add you to the early access list. Once the v0 is decent I'll pull you in.**
